YAML Explanation

The YAML section is controlling how the Quarto document is rendered. The title option sets the title of the webpage. The format section tells Quarto to create an HTML document. The option “code-fold: true” allows code to be hidden or shown, while “code-line-numbers: true” adds line numbers to the code. The execute section controls how code is handled when the document is rendered.

Critique of 3 Articles That Use Data and Visualization

Article 1: The Pudding - “Similes”

Link: https://pudding.cool/2026/05/similes/

One thing I really like about this article was how creative it went with the visuals and the overall design.

As for the visualization I choose, I wanted to give my opinions on the “# of Occurences” graph:

I like how the size and spacing of the circles immediately draw attention to the most common or influential words, such as “death” and “stone.” The use of a bubble chart also makes the graph visually engaging and helps communicate it’s insights in a more interactive and memorable way than a traditional bar chart would. In addition, the percentage scale along the bottom helps provide context for how predictable each word is within the dataset.

One thing that could be improved is readability. Because there are so many circles packed together, some labels become difficult to read or compare quickly. The graph also lacks a stronger explanation of what the bubble sizes specifically represent besides just “number of occurances,” which could make interpretation confusing.

Article 2: FiveThirtyEight — “The 52 Best — And Weirdest — Charts We Made In 2016”

Link: https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/the-52-best-and-weirdest-charts-we-made-in-2016/

This visualization does a really good job comparing the massive size differences between a human, a titanosaur, and Amphicoelias fragillimus. I especially like how the graph uses silhouettes and relative scaling instead of only numerical values because it makes the comparison much easier to understand at a glance. The use of color also helps separate the different species while still keeping the visualization clean. It is definitely a really creative way to present this infomration.

One thing that could be improved is the labeling and measurement system. While the visualization is visually impressive, it is slightly difficult to tell exactly how the heights and body lengths are being measured from the graph alone. Adding clearer axis labels or additional annotations could make the comparison easier to interpret. I also think that comparing the dinossaurs with something that people would be more familiar with, besides a human, could also help get a better idea of just how big they are.

Article 3: Our World in Data - “What is economic growth? And why is it so important?

Link: https://ourworldindata.org/what-is-economic-growth

This visualization does a great job showing the dramatic impact that the invention of the printing press had on book production in Western Europe. I especially liked the use of two different colors to separate handwritten manuscripts from printed books because it makes the transition between the two periods very easy to understand. The annotations and labels also help guide the reader through the historical context without overwhelming the graph with too much information.

One thing that could be improved is the scale of the graph in the earlier centuries. Because the increase in printed books becomes so large later in the chart, the handwritten manuscript values near the beginning become difficult to compare visually. Adding a secondary zoomed-in section or using a logarithmic scale could help make the earlier trends easier to interpret.
